# Strength tier audit – 13 October 2026

Every rule (550) was checked against the strength definitions shown on the Sources tab ("How syndromes are ranked") and in TIER_DEFS in js/data.js. The check was AI-assisted: five AI reviewers each took about 110 rules, working from the rules' cited sources and recorded risk figures, and proposed changes only where the evidence clearly placed a rule in a different tier. Each proposal was then checked for consistency across syndromes before being applied (8 were not applied, and 1 further change was needed – see below). This is an editorial check, not a clinical validation: the items under "For clinical review" in particular need a clinician's view.

## Definitions

A rule takes the higher of two measures; hallmark needs the first (or a defining feature present in most carriers).

| Strength | Points | Yield (of people with this tumour, how many have the syndrome) | Carrier risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hallmark | 10 | Pathognomonic or near-pathognomonic (about half or more have the syndrome), or a major diagnostic criterion rare outside it | Not enough on its own – except a defining feature present in most carriers and uncommon otherwise (e.g. >100 adenomas in FAP, multiple café-au-lait macules in NF1) |
| Strong | 6 | About 10–50% have the syndrome | Lifetime risk about 20% or more, or relative risk about 10 or more |
| Moderate | 3 | About 2–10% have the syndrome | Lifetime risk about 5–20%, or relative risk about 2–10 |
| Weak | 1 | Under about 2%, or unknown | Lifetime risk under about 5%, relative risk under about 2, or reported only in case reports or small series |

Additional principle adopted during the audit: in a syndrome usually recognised by its non-cancer features (polyposis, pigmentation, growth or skin), a common adult cancer on its own stays at most Moderate, whatever the carrier risk.
