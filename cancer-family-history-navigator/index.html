About this information

Genes
genes from the NICPR gene list (Appendix 1, June 2026; National Disease Registration Service) across conditions, plus a few Test Directory genes not on that list (marked on their pages). RAD51C and the Lynch genes are treated as fully in scope. Genes marked * carry NICPR notes; hover or tap for details.
Tests
NHS Genomic Test Directory, Rare and Inherited Disease eligibility criteria v9.1 (20 May 2026): Part XI (inherited cancer) and related chapters, with PanelApp panels.
Gene–tumour associations
Each association names its sources and, where published, a risk figure: UK guidance (UKCGG, BSG, NICE) first, then European guidance (ERN GENTURIS, EHTG, SIOPE), GeneReviews, ClinGen gene–disease validity and peer-reviewed risk studies. US guidance (NCCN, and US guidelines published in peer-reviewed journals such as ATA, AACR and ACMG) is also used; where it differs from UK guidance, UK guidance takes priority and the US position is shown as a difference. Variant-level notes are for information and do not change the ranking.
Surveillance
Summarised from UK guidance first, then European, then international; differences are listed. Confirm with the full guideline and local services before use.
Links
Each syndrome links to its GeneReviews chapter, OMIM entries, UK, European and US guidance, East Genomics patient information, GeNotes (NHS England Genomics Education Programme), ClinGen and CanVar-UK.
Checked
Information checked ; each syndrome shows its own last-reviewed date. Every source and version is listed on the Sources tab.

Clinical decision support only. Scores are relative, not probabilities, and do not replace clinical judgement, risk models such as CanRisk, or specialist advice. Nothing you enter leaves this browser.

Disclaimer

Who it is for
Healthcare professionals assessing a family history of cancer (for example in clinical genetics, oncology, breast or bowel services and primary care). It is not designed for patients or the public: if you are a patient or relative, please talk to your GP or genetics team rather than acting on these results.
What it does
From the cancers entered, it suggests germline cancer predisposition syndromes to consider, checks the inherited cancer criteria of the NHS National Genomic Test Directory, and gives guideline-based screening categories, each linked to its source.
What it does not do
It does not diagnose, calculate absolute cancer or carrier risks (the ranking scores are relative; only the Manchester score is a validated score), classify variants or replace CanRisk, specialist advice or the Test Directory itself – the Test Directory and your Genomic Laboratory Hub decide eligibility. It knows only what has been entered: earlier genetic test results, personal risk factors and cancers or pathology not entered are not taken into account.
Accuracy
Results are only as good as the family history entered – confirm reported diagnoses from histopathology or cancer registry records where possible. Its content was compiled and checked against the cited sources with AI assistance. Each syndrome page shows when its sources were last checked and whether a named clinician has reviewed it; most have not yet had clinical review. It may contain errors or omissions in its data or programming, and guidance and testing criteria change, so always check results against the primary sources linked on each page before making clinical decisions. Please report any errors.
